## Self-care for the first 2 to 3 days
For a sprain or strain, follow PRICE for the first 2 to 3 days: protect the injury, for example with a support; rest by stopping exercise and trying not to put weight on it; apply an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours; compress it with a bandage; and elevate it on a pillow as much as possible.

## What to avoid early on
Avoid heat, such as hot baths and heat packs, alcohol and massage for the first couple of days.

## Recovery and returning to exercise
Most sprains and strains improve within about 2 weeks, but strenuous exercise such as running should be avoided for up to 8 weeks, and severe sprains and strains can take months to get back to normal.

## When to get help
Get help from NHS 111, or go to an urgent treatment centre, if the injury is very painful or the pain is getting worse, there is a large amount of swelling or bruising or it is getting worse, you cannot put any weight on it or walk more than a few steps, it feels very stiff or is difficult to move, it is not feeling any better after treating it yourself, or you also have a very high temperature or feel hot, cold or shivery, which could be an infection. See a GP if you think you have a sprain or strain and it is getting worse or not feeling better after treating it yourself. Call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack when you had the injury, the injured part has changed shape or is pointing at an odd angle, it is numb, tingling or has pins and needles, or the skin around it has changed colour (such as blue or grey) or is cold to touch, because you may have broken a bone.
