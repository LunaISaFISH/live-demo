## Warning signs and symptoms
Exercise-related cardiovascular events are often preceded by warning signs or symptoms. Signs and symptoms suggestive of cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease include, at rest or during activity: pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw, arms or other areas that may result from ischaemia; shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion; dizziness or fainting; orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea; ankle swelling; palpitations or a rapid heartbeat; intermittent claudication (leg pain on walking that eases with rest); a known heart murmur; or unusual fatigue or shortness of breath with usual activities.

## What to do when symptoms appear
Under the screening algorithm, a physically active person who develops signs or symptoms of cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease should discontinue exercise and seek medical clearance before resuming exercise of any intensity. A physically inactive person with such signs or symptoms should seek medical clearance before starting an exercise program, regardless of the intensity.
